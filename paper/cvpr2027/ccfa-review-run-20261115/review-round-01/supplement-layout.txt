 234   6. Failure Boundaries and Research Scope
 235   Two implemented coding prototypes test the gap between a
 236   diagnostic and a realizable policy. A spatial selector ex-
 237   changes occupied same-channel, equal-outdegree events
 238   and predicts consequences using 1,304 fitted coefficients.
 239   Six matched controls include local, non-graph, true-graph,
 240   permuted-graph and topology-descriptor conditions. It pre-
 241   serves first-site counts and amplitude histograms, but fails
 242   full-network cost and prefix criteria. A temporal selector
 243   uses 584 coefficients and source-verified codeword alterna-
 244   tives. It preserves first-site T = 2 output but likewise fails
 245   the joint cost and accuracy criteria. These are counterexam-
 246   ples to treating local invariants as deployment guarantees,
 247   not impossibility results for future encoders.
 248       The empirical conclusion is therefore narrower and more
 249   useful than a ranking of unsuccessful losses. A norm can
 250   improve without preserving the signed decision condition;
 251   local endpoint equality can hold without preserving the state
 252   trajectory; and local activity can decrease while global activ-
 253   ity increases. Stronger predictive or algorithmic claims need
 254   replication on additional teachers and tasks with a frozen rule.
 255   The present evidence supports these identified distinctions
 256   in the stated networks and codes.
