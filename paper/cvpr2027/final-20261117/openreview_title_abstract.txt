TITLE
Beyond Reconstruction: Auditing Decision and Temporal Fidelity in Graph-to-Spike Conversion

ABSTRACT
ANN-to-SNN conversion commonly targets accurate local reconstruction. Yet in graph classifiers, a smaller reconstruction error can worsen a classification decision, and even an identical local decoded endpoint need not preserve downstream behavior. To investigate these failures, we introduce a finite-event audit that applies realizable event interventions to frozen graph neural networks for visual superpixel classification while preserving the graph and pretrained weights. First, among single-event interventions that reduce first-tick graph-message reconstruction error, 40.9% on MNIST and 31.9% on CIFAR-10 worsen the teacher-class margin. Directional margin analysis explains why reconstruction improvements need not favor the original decision. Second, we exchange two-tick codewords with identical local decoded endpoints and event counts but different intermediate prefixes and absolute amplitudes. Exchanging all eligible codewords for their amplitude-reducing counterparts changes predictions on 6 of 128 MNIST and 16 of 128 CIFAR-10 training graphs, while increasing whole-network event counts by 3.41% and 4.99%, respectively. Complementary calibration and residual-centering studies illustrate the implications for conversion design. Together, these findings establish the need to evaluate decision fidelity, prefix-wise behavior, and whole-network activity alongside reconstruction accuracy.
